The California Electronic Waste Recycling Act

Key Provisions of California's E-Waste Law

Enacted in 2003 and updated regularly, the California Electronic Waste Recycling Act (SB 20/SB 50) establishes one of the nation's most comprehensive e-waste recycling programs:

  • Universal Waste Rule: Classifies most consumer electronics as "universal waste," banning them from landfills
  • Advanced Recycling Fee: Consumers pay a fee at purchase that funds recycling programs (visible on receipts as "CA Electronic Waste Recycling Fee")
  • Manufacturer Responsibility: Requires manufacturers to establish and fund take-back programs
  • Free Recycling: Mandates free drop-off for covered electronic devices for all California residents
  • Data Security: Requires recyclers to provide data destruction services or guidance

Covered Devices: Televisions, computer monitors, laptops, tablets, desktop computers, and most display devices containing screens larger than 4 inches diagonally.

Why Throwing Electronics in the Trash is Illegal & Harmful

Improper e-waste disposal poses serious risks and violates California law:

  • Legal Penalties: Fines up to $2,500 for individuals and $25,000 for businesses
  • Toxic Leaching: Lead, mercury, cadmium, and other heavy metals contaminate soil and groundwater
  • Fire Hazard: Lithium-ion batteries can ignite in trash compactors or landfills
  • Resource Waste: Valuable materials like gold, silver, copper, and rare earth metals are lost forever
  • Data Vulnerability: Personal information remains on devices disposed in regular trash
  • Environmental Justice: Much e-waste is illegally exported to developing countries with poor safety standards

What Happens to Your Recycled Electronics

The E-Waste Recycling Process in San Diego County

When you drop off electronics at certified facilities, they undergo a meticulous process to maximize material recovery and ensure environmental protection:

1

Data Destruction

Certified data wiping or physical destruction of storage devices following NIST 800-88 standards

2

Manual Disassembly

Skilled technicians separate components: batteries, circuit boards, plastics, metals, glass

3

Component Recovery

Valuable materials extracted: gold from connectors, copper from wiring, rare earth metals

4

Material Processing

Plastics shredded for reuse, metals smelted, glass cleaned for new screen manufacturing

San Diego's Success: Local facilities recover approximately 95% of materials from electronics, with less than 5% sent to specialized hazardous waste facilities for safe disposal.

Accepted & Prohibited E-Waste Items

Televisions

All types: CRT, LCD, LED, plasma. Free recycling at certified centers.

Computers

Desktops, laptops, tablets, servers. Remove personal data first.

Mobile Devices

Cell phones, smartphones, tablets. Consider donation if functional.

Peripherals

Printers, keyboards, mice, cables. Often accepted for free.

Prohibited or Special Handling Items

  • Household appliances: Refrigerators, microwaves, air conditioners (different recycling stream)
  • Hazardous components: Lithium-ion batteries over 300 watt-hours (special handling)
  • Business equipment: Large servers, medical equipment (may require special appointment)
  • Contaminated items: Electronics damaged by water, fire, or biological hazards
  • Non-electronic items: Furniture, media (CDs/DVDs), manuals (recycle separately)
